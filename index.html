01 · OVERVIEW

Why this project exists.

A penetration test does more than list open ports. It investigates whether an exposed service can be exploited, verifies the resulting access, and documents the impact and defensive actions supported by the evidence.

PROBLEM

Exposed services can create risk.

A computer may run multiple network services. If one uses vulnerable software, it may provide a path to unauthorized access.

AIM

Validate one attack path.

Scan the authorized lab target, investigate the vsftpd 2.3.4 service, test the known backdoor path, and verify the privileges actually obtained.

APPROACH

Prove, then explain.

Use Nmap for discovery, Metasploit for controlled validation, Meterpreter for verification, and Wireshark to observe related network traffic.

02 · OBJECTIVES

What the practical set out to do.

The engagement followed a sequence from preparing the lab to documenting the verified result.

01–02 / DISCOVER

Prepare & enumerate

Confirm lab connectivity, scan TCP ports and identify the software versions exposed by the target.

03–04 / VALIDATE

Research & exploit

Research the vsftpd 2.3.4 backdoor issue and use the corresponding Metasploit module only against the lab target.

05–06 / DOCUMENT

Verify & assess

Check the session's privilege level, record system and network details, observe associated TCP traffic, and recommend remediation.

06 · ANALYSIS

What the evidence means.

Ports, packets and vulnerabilities are different things. The analysis keeps them separate and interprets each result in context.

PORTS

23 open TCP ports

These are network service endpoints discovered by Nmap. An open port alone does not prove that a service is vulnerable or exploitable.

VULNERABILITY

vsftpd 2.3.4

The service version was associated with CVE-2011-2523. The module's banner-based check was a lead; the successful exploit output validated this path in the lab.

PACKETS

Six filtered TCP packets

The Wireshark screenshot shows traffic between Kali port 4444 and target port 56350. These are packets from an established session, not six separate attack methods.

08 · REMEDIATION

What should be fixed.

The purpose of a penetration test is not only to prove a weakness, but to give the system owner a path to reduce risk.

FIX 01

Patch or remove

Replace vulnerable vsftpd builds with trusted patched software and verify package integrity.

FIX 02

Reduce exposure

Disable services that are not required and restrict exposed ports with firewall rules.

FIX 03

Least privilege

Run network-facing services with minimal permissions and isolate them from sensitive resources.

FIX 04

Secure file transfer

Restrict legacy FTP and use secure, authenticated transfer mechanisms where appropriate.

FIX 05

Monitor

Review logs, unexpected listeners and outbound connections for signs of compromise.

FIX 06

Retest

Verify the fix and assess other exposed services individually; they were not validated by this exploit.

09 · CONCLUSION

From scanning to demonstrated impact.

The project began by confirming lab connectivity and using Nmap to identify exposed services. The FTP service on port 21 was identified as vsftpd 2.3.4, leading to research of CVE-2011-2523 and selection of the corresponding Metasploit module. The exploit successfully opened a Meterpreter session, and getuid confirmed root-level access. System and network details were recorded, and Wireshark showed six packets associated with the established session. The result demonstrates one successful attack path in a deliberately vulnerable lab VM. The practical reinforces the importance of patching, service minimization, least privilege, network restrictions and monitoring.
